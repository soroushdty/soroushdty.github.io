# Research Overview

As a physician-scientist and PhD researcher in Biomedical Informatics at Arizona State University, my work bridges clinical domain knowledge, health wearables, and machine learning research to build health AI systems that clinicians and patients can safely trust.

---

<div class="space-y-16 py-6">

<section id="wearable-biosignals" class="scroll-mt-24">
  <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 dark:text-white mb-4 flex items-center gap-3">
    <span class="w-3 h-8 bg-rose-500 rounded-full inline-block"></span>
    Wearable Biosignals &amp; Cardiovascular Modeling
  </h2>
  <p class="text-lg text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">
    Continuous physiological monitoring using consumer and clinical-grade wearable sensors offers transformative potential for remote patient management, especially in high-risk populations such as cardio-oncology. Translating raw multimodal biosignals into actionable clinical biomarkers requires grounding sensor data in physiological principles and cardiovascular models.
  </p>
  <div class="bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800/50 rounded-2xl p-6 border border-gray-200 dark:border-gray-700 space-y-3">
    <h3 class="font-semibold text-gray-900 dark:text-white">Key Research Focus Areas:</h3>
    <ul class="list-disc list-inside text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 space-y-2">
      <li>Multimodal biosignal processing and feature extraction from continuous PPG and ECG waveforms.</li>
      <li>Cardiovascular and physiological modeling for remote monitoring in cardio-oncology (SHANDHI Lab, AHA-funded).</li>
      <li>Digital biomarker translation pipelines and evaluation frameworks for trustworthy clinical AI in wearable cardiology.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section id="trustworthy-clinical-llms" class="scroll-mt-24">
  <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 dark:text-white mb-4 flex items-center gap-3">
    <span class="w-3 h-8 bg-primary-600 rounded-full inline-block"></span>
    Trustworthy Clinical LLMs
  </h2>
  <p class="text-lg text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">
    Large language models offer unprecedented capabilities in processing clinical documentation, summarizing complex medical histories, and supporting decision-making. However, deployment in real-world clinical environments requires rigorous evaluation frameworks to detect hallucinations, measure calibration, and assess domain safety.
  </p>
  <div class="bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800/50 rounded-2xl p-6 border border-gray-200 dark:border-gray-700 space-y-3">
    <h3 class="font-semibold text-gray-900 dark:text-white">Key Research Focus Areas:</h3>
    <ul class="list-disc list-inside text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 space-y-2">
      <li>Automated hallucination detection in clinical summaries and consultations.</li>
      <li>Context-aware LLMs for sensitive health data classification and privacy protection.</li>
      <li>Evaluation metrics aligned with real-world physician workflow requirements.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section id="evidence-grounding" class="scroll-mt-24">
  <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 dark:text-white mb-4 flex items-center gap-3">
    <span class="w-3 h-8 bg-secondary-500 rounded-full inline-block"></span>
    Evidence Grounding
  </h2>
  <p class="text-lg text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">
    For health AI output to be actionable in clinical care, every claim must be explicitly grounded in authoritative biomedical evidence, electronic health record (EHR) data, or peer-reviewed literature.
  </p>
  <div class="bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800/50 rounded-2xl p-6 border border-gray-200 dark:border-gray-700 space-y-3">
    <h3 class="font-semibold text-gray-900 dark:text-white">Key Research Focus Areas:</h3>
    <ul class="list-disc list-inside text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 space-y-2">
      <li>Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) verification for clinical practice guidelines.</li>
      <li>Auditability tools to trace AI outputs back to source EHR clinical notes.</li>
      <li>Computational platforms (e.g. EviTrace) for evidence attribution.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section id="fhir-health-data" class="scroll-mt-24">
  <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 dark:text-white mb-4 flex items-center gap-3">
    <span class="w-3 h-8 bg-emerald-500 rounded-full inline-block"></span>
    Interoperable Health Data &amp; Granular Segmentation
  </h2>
  <p class="text-lg text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">
    Health Information Exchanges (HIEs) require robust data segmentation to protect sensitive patient records (such as substance use disorder data under 42 CFR Part 2) while ensuring interoperability across care settings.
  </p>
  <div class="bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800/50 rounded-2xl p-6 border border-gray-200 dark:border-gray-700 space-y-3">
    <h3 class="font-semibold text-gray-900 dark:text-white">Key Research Focus Areas:</h3>
    <ul class="list-disc list-inside text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 space-y-2">
      <li>FHIR-based granular data segmentation (SHARES project, NIDA-funded).</li>
      <li>Standardized terminology mapping (SNOMED-CT, LOINC, RxNorm, ICD-10).</li>
      <li>Scalable deployment architectures for privacy-preserving health data exchange.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>
